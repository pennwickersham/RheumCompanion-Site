Two halves that need each other.

Most tracking apps give you a place to write things down and leave you there. Most reference apps explain your disease without knowing anything about you. RheumCompanion does both, because the question you actually bring to an appointment sits between them: is this normal for my condition, or is this my condition getting worse?

Built for the conditions it names.

Rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, psoriatic arthritis, Sjögren's syndrome, ankylosing spondylitis, gout, scleroderma, vasculitis, fibromyalgia — and other autoimmune and inflammatory conditions.

If your diagnosis is on that list, the tracking fields, the encyclopedia entries and the medication reference were written with it in mind. Nothing here is a general wellness template with rheumatology vocabulary pasted over it.

Every claim carries its source.

Medication information comes from FDA drug labels. Clinical content is drawn from published research indexed in PubMed. Where the app tells you something, it shows you where that came from, so you can take it to your rheumatologist and they can check it.

That is deliberate. A patient-facing app that speaks confidently without citation is asking you to trust it more than it has earned.
